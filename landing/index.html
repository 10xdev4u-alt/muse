A journal that talks back.
Offline.

Muse is an Android journal with a real language model living inside it. Write what's on your mind; it reflects back with questions instead of advice. Everything runs on your phone. Airplane mode is the intended way to use it.

100% on-device inference no accounts no api keys biometric lock MIT + open source

Three moves, no internet

One 731 MB model downloads over Wi-Fi the first time you open Mind. After that, the network is dead weight — Muse never calls home.

01

Pour it out

Type raw. The words stay yours — plain text, saved locally, rendered exactly as written.

02

Get reflected

Muse mirrors your words and asks one open question. It doesn't lecture, diagnose, or cheerlead.

03

See your day honestly

Once daily, it reads your real task history and leaves three grounded prompts about how today actually went.

The privacy stance is structural

Not a policy page. Architecture.

QuestionMuse's answer
Where do my thoughts go?Your phone's local database. Included in its encrypted-at-rest app storage.
Where does inference run?On-device CPU, via llama.cpp. Unloadable in one tap.
What leaves the device?One model download, once, that you start yourself. Nothing else, ever.
Who can read my journal?Whoever passes the biometric or PIN lock. Same as the rest of the app.
Analytics? Telemetry?No. Not even on this landing page.

Built in the open

Every feature lands as a reviewed PR against a tracked issue. Research first, code second.
