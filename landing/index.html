01

Capability matrix.

What Muse does today, shipped — not promised.

CAP_001

Streaming reflection

Type a thought; tokens stream back at ~190ms cadence on budget hardware. Persona contract: direct answers, no questions back, no lecturing.

CAP_002

Daily grounded review

Once per calendar day, reads your real task data (completed / pending / overdue) and leaves three numbered reflection prompts in your history.

CAP_003

Session history

Every exchange persists as chat-shaped rows. Browse by day, reopen, delete with undo. What is on screen is literally what is in the database.

CAP_004

Verified acquisition

One-time 731 MB fetch: resumable, Wi-Fi-gated by default, SHA256-checked against a hash committed in this repository before load.

CAP_005

RAM discipline

Model unloads after 60 s backgrounded. Reloads transparently on next use. A journal must not hold a gigabyte while you answer texts.

CAP_006

Inheritance

Biometric lock, trash with undo, JSON backup, CSV export, widgets, reminders — all inherited from the task-manager foundation and applied to your journals.

08

Interrogation.

Questions we expect. Expand at will — these are native disclosure elements, not a widget library.

Is my journal really never uploaded?
There is no upload path. Inference is in-process via llama.cpp; storage is a local Room database. The only network call the AI feature can make is the one-time model download you explicitly start, and its bytes are checksum-verified against a hash committed in the repository before they are ever loaded. Airplane mode is a supported configuration, not an error state.
How smart is a 1.2B model, honestly?
It is a sharp, fast reflector — not GPT. Expect short, grounded replies tuned by a persona contract: mirror, respond concretely, stay under ~120 words. For deep reasoning you still want a large cloud model; Muse is for thinking alongside something private that lives in your pocket.
Why did you build a custom JNI bridge?
The published Kotlin SDK shipped its native library without JNI exports, so it could not load on any Android device (documented in upstream issue #530 and native-bridge/README.md). We generate the missing 85 trampolines from upstream's own C header. Once upstream fixes packaging, the bridge retires.
What does it cost?
Nothing. No tiers, no subscription, no account. The runtime is Apache-2.0, the app is MIT, the weights carry Liquid AI's LFM Open License. You pay bandwidth for one download.
Can I see how it was built?
Yes — that is the point. 28 reviewed pull requests, 39 tracked issues, architecture decision records, benchmark methods, and a journey log covering every crash are all public. Start at docs/journey/.
What happened to the todo-app part?
It is still here — Muse grew out of YATA, a full offline task manager. Your tasks power the daily review; your journals sit beside them under the same lock. One app, two rooms: do, then think.
