A journal that talks back. Offline.

Muse is an Android journal with a real language model living inside it. Write what's on your mind — it reflects back with questions instead of advice. Everything runs on your phone. Airplane mode is the intended way to use it.

Three moves. No internet.

One 731 MB model arrives over Wi-Fi the first time you open Mind. After that the radio is dead weight — Muse never calls home.

01 / POUR

Pour it out

Type raw. Your words stay yours — plain text, saved locally, rendered exactly as written.

02 / REFLECT

Get reflected

The model mirrors what you wrote and asks one open question. It doesn't lecture, diagnose, or cheerlead.

03 / GROUND

See your day honestly

Once daily it reads your real task history and leaves three grounded prompts about how today actually went.

Privacy is structural

Not a policy page. Architecture.

QuestionMuse's answer
Where do my thoughts go?Your phone's local database, inside Android's encrypted app storage. Included in the app's own backup/export.
Where does inference run?On-device CPU via llama.cpp. Unloadable in one tap from Settings.
What leaves the device?One model download, once, started by you. SHA256-pinned and verified before load. Nothing else, ever.
Who can read my journal?Whoever passes the biometric or PIN lock — same gate as every other screen.
Analytics? Telemetry?No. Not on this page either — zero third-party requests, fonts included.

Built in the open, shipped with receipts

Every feature lands as a reviewed pull request against a tracked issue. Research first, code second, metrics public.
